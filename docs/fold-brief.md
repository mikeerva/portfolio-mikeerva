# FOLD case study — strategic direction

Read this before making any changes to the Fold case study. This document replaces the previous Fold brief.

## My view of the project

Fold already has a strong visual identity. The photography, cream / black / signal-orange palette, condensed typography, folded-corner language and editorial pacing make it more distinctive than a generic designer portfolio.

The problem is not that Fold needs more visual content. The problem is that the design logic is not immediately clear enough. A recruiter, client or creative director should understand within one or two minutes:

1. What Fold is.
2. What Mike did.
3. What the design problem was.
4. What the central idea is.
5. How that idea becomes a repeatable identity system.

Keep the current visual direction. Improve the explanation, hierarchy and proof.

## Main strategic direction

The case study should move through this sequence:

`Brief → Idea → System → Applications → Closing`

The story should not feel like a collection of beautiful mockups. It should feel like one clear physical idea becoming a complete hospitality identity.

The central sentence is:

> Fold is a verb before it is a logo.

The central system is:

> Close → Fold → Signal

This should become the clearest design principle in the case study.

## What the case study needs

### 1. A clearer brief near the beginning

After the hero, add a short, highly scannable brief. Do not write a long essay.

It should communicate:

- Project: Fold
- Category: Brand Identity / Hospitality
- Year: 2026
- Location: Athens
- Type: Personal concept
- Mike’s role: strategy, identity, wordmark, art direction, packaging, print, spatial direction, digital and motion

Also include three short blocks:

**Context**
Neighbourhood bakery and specialty coffee, all day.

**Challenge**
Create a recognisable identity that works equally well on a receipt, cup, package and storefront without becoming ornamental.

**Approach**
Build the identity around one physical action: the fold.

The hero itself should stay cinematic and minimal. Do not overload it with information.

### 2. A stronger explanation of the idea

Keep the existing idea:

> Built around the way the city moves.

Keep the behaviour sequence:

`Grab / Carry / Open / Fold / Eat / Go`

Explain the idea once, clearly:

> The fold is not a graphic added to the brand. It is the action that connects it: the package closes, the corner shows the signal colour, and the customer opens it again.

Do not repeat the same explanation in every chapter. State the full idea here, then refer to it briefly later.

The city photography should support movement and use, not only still-life presentation.

Do not add new photography or video unless Mike provides or explicitly approves it.

### 3. A visible identity system section

Add a compact section that proves Fold is a system, not just a logo and a colour palette.

Use five rules:

1. **The signal** — the orange/red colour appears through the fold.
2. **The pattern** — angular orange/red/deep-red planes fill larger surfaces and digital screens.
3. **The wordmark** — compressed, connected and released.
4. **The materials** — paper, kraft, concrete, ink and sunlight.
5. **The type** — display typography for presence, mono typography for operations.

Show each rule with a small visual and one short caption. Keep the section editorial and tactile. Avoid generic icon cards or SaaS-style components.

Include the existing palette and the three existing typefaces where appropriate. Do not introduce new colours or fonts.

### 4. A sharper wordmark rationale

Keep the current O → L and L → D construction, but make the reasoning more direct:

> Built by compression and release. O → L: two forms press until they share an edge. L → D: the relationship opens again and the word breathes. The word does what the brand does — close, open, move.

Keep the sticker, cup, bag and receipt applications. Captions should communicate function and scale, for example:

- Sticker / signal accent
- Cup / 40 mm
- Bag stamp / one colour
- Receipt / 14 mm, small-size reproduction

### 5. Packaging as proof of the system

Keep the current packaging photography and range. Present it through the simple sequence:

`CLOSE → FOLD → SIGNAL`

Explain each action in one short line:

- Close — protects the product.
- Fold — makes the brand action.
- Signal — shows where to open.

The viewer should understand why the same behaviour appears on the cup, box, bag, napkin and wrap. The objects do not need to look identical; they need to feel related.

### 6. Physical space as a scale change

Show that the same system scales from a small package to a building.

Use a concise line such as:

> At the scale of a building, the fold frames entrances, corners and counters without decorating.

Keep the storefront, counter and A-frame imagery. Make the relationship between packaging, signage and architecture explicit.

### 7. Print and menu as functional design

Keep the menu, receipt, loyalty card, poster and tee applications.

Explain that the system remains recognisable even when the application is mostly typography and paper:

> Mostly type and paper — the corner adds recognition without interrupting the information.

Where useful, use captions or subtle annotations to show hierarchy, sections, price alignment and signal-corner placement.

### 8. Digital should extend the physical idea

Keep both the social feed and the interactive Order Ahead prototype.

Do not remove or simplify the working prototype. It is one of the strongest parts of the project because it proves that the identity can become an interaction, not just a static graphic.

Frame the digital section with:

**The same behaviour, faster.**

> Online, the fold becomes a crop, a transition or a reveal.

The Order Ahead prototype should be presented as a translation of the physical fold into a digital interaction.

### 9. Motion should explain the behaviour

Keep the existing motion direction and demos. The three principles should be:

`Close · Fold · Open`

Motion should demonstrate the brand idea rather than exist as decoration. Make sure the fold reveal visibly exposes the signal colour and mark beneath it.

### 10. A clear closing summary

The ending should summarise the project in one strong statement:

> One physical action, one complete identity. It closes. It signals. It opens again.

Follow it with a compact scope summary:

Strategy · Identity · Wordmark · Packaging · Print · Space · Digital · Motion

The closing should make it obvious that Fold is a complete identity system.

## What not to do

- Do not redesign the Fold brand.
- Do not change the existing palette, fonts or overall art direction.
- Do not turn the case study into a generic grid or agency template.
- Do not add long paragraphs.
- Do not repeat the same explanation in every chapter.
- Do not add new photos or videos without Mike’s approval.
- Do not hide important project information behind interaction.
- Do not remove the interactive Order Ahead prototype.
- Do not modify other projects or unrelated global site components.

## Priority order

If time is limited, prioritise in this order:

1. Clear brief and role near the beginning.
2. Identity System section with the five rules.
3. Stronger explanation of Close → Fold → Signal.
4. Better captions and functional annotations.
5. Clear digital and motion connection.
6. Closing summary.
7. Spacing, loading and motion bugs.

## Final quality test

The Fold case study is successful if someone can scan only the hero, section headings and captions and still understand:

- what Fold is
- what the challenge was
- what Mike designed
- what the fold means
- how the system works across touchpoints
- why the project is more than a logo and mockups

The goal is not to add more content. The goal is to make the existing work feel intentional, strategic and complete.
